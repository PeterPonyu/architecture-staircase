\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.}
Which sub-network of a transformer must be \emph{trained} for a hard sub-function to
be acquired, and which sub-network \emph{carries} that sub-function once training is
done? Optimizer-centric accounts predict that orthogonalizing updates (e.g., Muon)
should flatten sequential simplicity bias and reorganize acquisition order; fixed-ansatz
accounts predict a low-dimensional, task-determined update subspace independent of the
optimizer. Neither framing cleanly separates \emph{training necessity} from
\emph{trained computation}, nor tests whether the acquisition geometry those probes act
on is itself optimizer-set.

\textbf{Methods.}
We study controlled Boolean degree-staircase targets on tiny two-layer transformers
(${\sim}400$k parameters), where Walsh-basis structure makes per-degree acquisition
directly measurable, with a companion in-context induction task for timing and subspace
probes. Across AdamW, Muon, and stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDM) we apply freeze probes
(training-necessity; SGDM arms are fit-gated and treated as uninformative). Trained-model
mean-ablation (trained computation) is run under AdamW and Muon only. Geometry uses an
analysis-plan two-one-sided equivalence test (TOST) of the staircase index (SI) at the smallest effect size of interest (SESOI)
$\Delta=1.5$ SI units (uncorrected and Holm-corrected), plus depth/width ablations, a
24-configuration scale grid, and a principal-component analysis (PCA) subspace audit. The multiplicity-safe sensitivity
margin $\pm2.0$ SI units is the smallest corrected bound at which all three pairs pass
(Holm), not a second pre-specified primary. One-sample tests against $\mathrm{SI}=1$ are
robustness checks only.

\textbf{Results.}
Freezing and ablation dissociate. Freezing (training-necessity) is scoped primarily to
the AdamW--Muon contrast: attention must be trained for degree~4 under AdamW at
$d{=}128$ (tightening to 8/8 seeds at $L{=}24$, $d{=}256$), while under Muon a
layer-1 two-path \emph{training-time} redundancy under the freeze protocol leaves
degree~4 intact at $d{=}128$ (both-frozen floor $+0.002$) and reverses at the larger
audit; SGDM freeze arms are fit-gated and uninformative for this probe. Ablation
(trained computation): layer-1 multilayer perceptron (MLP) ranks highest in degree-4 selectivity under both
optimizers (point estimates $+0.37$ AdamW / $+0.34$ Muon, robust in sign), but absolute
$95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) include zero at $n{=}8$; the paired within-seed MLP-minus-attention contrast
excludes zero only under Muon ($+0.141$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.058,+0.224]$; AdamW paired
$+0.090$ $[-0.159,+0.315]$). The order those probes act on is not optimizer-set on the primary equal-weight
staircase: pre-specified TOST at $\Delta=1.5$ passes uncorrected for all pairs
(Muon pairs marginal under Holm); as sensitivity, all pairs are equivalent within
$\pm2.0$ SI under Holm ($p\le0.006$). SI sits far above $\mathrm{SI}=1$
(robustness $t(14)=5.50,6.34,5.28$). Depth $\geq2$ is required; optimizers move only
timing (AdamW $1.6$--$3.7\times$ vs Muon on best-resolved cells; SGDM up to
${\sim}12\times$ on mixed-cadence grids; matched-coarse bounds are lower). An
induction-subspace probe returns a bounded null on idealized ansatz directions.

\textbf{Conclusions.}
Training necessity is optimizer-dependent (AdamW vs Muon, with explicit scope tags).
The finished degree-4-selective locus ranks first at the layer-1 MLP under both
optimizers in point estimate (absolute CIs include zero at $n{=}8$; paired MLP$>$attn
interval support only under Muon). The primary-staircase acquisition order is not
optimizer-set (with SGDM order/fit boundaries on some target families). No new
learning-order mechanism is required: the optimizer is a clock, not a compass. The
contribution is a controlled, multi-seed dissociation with full open logs, not a new
architecture or leaderboard result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A central question in understanding deep learning is what determines the shape
of how a network learns: which sub-functions are acquired in which order, how
low-dimensional the learning trajectory is, and which architectural component carries
the hardest piece. Two influential framings answer this differently. The
optimizer-centric view holds that the optimizer sets the acquisition order:
transformers acquire low-degree token interactions before high-degree ones because stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
and its variants have an implicit simplicity bias
\citep{rende2024distributional,bhattamishra2023simplicity,vasudeva2025lowsensitivity},
and that an idealized orthogonalizing update (Muon \citep{jordan2024muon}) should remove the sequential
simplicity bias by spectrally flattening the gradient directions
\citep{dragutinovic2026usemuon}. The fixed-ansatz view holds that emergence
lives in a fixed, low-dimensional subspace of update directions that is determined by the
task and independent of the optimizer \citep{musat2025inductionheads}. Both views
attribute the shape of emergence to something other than the static architecture.

The broader context is the grokking literature
\citep{power2022grokking,nanda2023progress,liu2023omnigrok,kumar2024lazytorich}, in which generalization
emerges long after overfitting and is accompanied by measurable circuit-level changes;
recent in-venue work reconciles grokking with statistical learning theory through norm- and
stability-based generalization bounds \citep{neucom2026grokking}, to which we add a
complementary attribution of emergence geometry to architecture versus optimizer.
Induction heads are the canonical circuit behind in-context learning
\citep{olsson2022induction}; their abrupt, data-dependent emergence has been traced
mechanistically \citep{reddy2024mechanistic} and characterised on Markov data as the
formation of statistical induction heads \citep{edelman2024statinduction}. Phase transitions in Boolean function learning, where SGD
shows hidden progress until a sudden phase transition, have been studied in parity tasks
\citep{barak2022hidden,abbe2023leap}, and staircase / leap-complexity theory
\citep{abbe2021staircase,abbe2023leap} formalizes how hierarchical structure guides
sequential acquisition. Transformer depth and expressivity have been studied through
depth-separation arguments \citep{sanford2023transformers}. The implicit bias of
different optimizers, including the $\ell_\infty$ norm constraint of Adam with
decoupled weight decay (AdamW)
\citep{xie2024adamw}, the late-phase implicit bias that provably induces grokking
\citep{lyu2024dichotomy}, and the large-deviation account of SGD's implicit bias
\citep{neucom2026sgdbias}, shapes generalization independently of learning order.

\paragraph{Our argument.} We argue for a two-probe dissociation. Asking which
sub-network must be \emph{trained} for the hardest component to be acquired (a causal,
training-necessity question) and asking which sub-network \emph{carries} that component
in the finished network (a localization question about the trained computation) are
different questions, and they have different answers: the first is optimizer-dependent
(AdamW vs Muon, scoped), while the second is a point-estimate regularity across
optimizers with interval support only where the paired contrast excludes zero. The natural objection is that either answer could be
confounded with the acquisition geometry itself---that what looks like component
ownership is just what a particular optimizer happens to learn first. We address this
directly by holding the architecture fixed and varying the optimizer across three
families with markedly different update geometry (AdamW, the orthogonalizing Muon, and
SGD with momentum (SGDM)): an effect that is genuinely architecture- or computation-owned should survive
this swap, while an optimizer-owned effect should not. The acquisition order itself
survives it (pre-specified equivalence test); the training-necessity pattern does not;
the trained-computation locus does. We are explicit about which legs are measured
directly across optimizers and which are scoped to a single optimizer or remain a null.
The cross-optimizer timing sweep enters as that timing control for the
dissociation, not as a separate contribution.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We group the results into the ownership dissociation, the
geometry it sits on, the boundary conditions, and the supporting theory.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The two-probe ownership dissociation}
    (Sections~\ref{sec:ownership},~\ref{sec:ablation}).
    Freezing and ablation answer different questions and dissociate cleanly.
    Freezing (training-necessity) is optimizer-dependent: training attention is
    necessary for degree-4 acquisition under AdamW at $d=128$ (tightening to 8/8
    seeds at the $L=24$, $d=256$ audit), while under Muon a verified layer-1
    two-path \emph{training-time} redundancy leaves degree~4 intact at $d=128$
    (both-frozen floor control at degree-4 correlation $+0.002$)---and the Muon
    pattern itself is config-dependent, reversing at $L=24$, $d=256$ where
    attention-freeze rescues degree~4 (rank index $+1.000$, 8/8 seeds); SGDM freeze
    arms sit at a median degree-4 floor with only sparse seed-level acquisition
    ($n{=}5$ per arm), so they are fit-gated and uninformative for the
    training-necessity probe.
    Ablation (trained computation): the degree-4-selective carrier ranks first at
    the layer-1 multilayer perceptron (MLP) under both optimizers (point estimates
    $+0.369$ AdamW / $+0.344$ Muon, largest selectivity of any arm under each;
    95\% confidence intervals (CIs) include zero at $n=8$ because degree-4
    acquisition is bimodal; the paired within-seed MLP-minus-attention contrast
    excludes zero only under Muon ($+0.141$, 95\% CI $[+0.058$, $+0.224]$; AdamW
    paired $+0.090$ $[-0.159$, $+0.315]$)), while trained layer-1 attention
    supports degrees~2--4 collectively rather than degree-4-selectively. Read jointly:
    under AdamW the attention pathway must be \emph{trained} for degree~4 to be
    acquired at all, yet the degree-4-selective component of the finished function is
    \emph{deposited} in the layer-1 MLP.
  \item \textbf{The acquisition geometry is optimizer-equivalent (analysis-plan TOST)}
    (Sections~\ref{sec:si},~\ref{sec:arch},~\ref{sec:timing}). The pre-specified
    analysis is a two-one-sided equivalence test (TOST)
    \citep{schuirmann1987tost,lakens2017equivalence} on the staircase index (SI): all
    three optimizer pairs are equivalent within the analysis-plan smallest effect size of interest (SESOI) of $1.5$ SI
    units at uncorrected $\alpha=0.05$ (Muon pairs marginal under Holm); as sensitivity,
    all pairs are equivalent within $\pm2.0$ SI under Holm ($p\le0.006$).
    Robustness point-null check: SI is above $\mathrm{SI}=1$ under every optimizer
    ($n=15$ each; one-sample $t(14)=5.50$, $6.34$, $5.28$ for AdamW, Muon, SGDM).
    The order reflects the target basis and requires depth $\geq2$. Separately, the
    induction timing grid (Section~\ref{sec:timing}; not the SI TOST) shows the
    optimizer advancing emergence without reorganizing that order (Muon
    $1.6$--$3.7\times$ over AdamW on best-resolved cells; ${\sim}6.9$--$12\times$
    over SGDM)---the timing control for the dissociation. Three fresh random draws
    of the monomial sets confirm the order is not a single-task-instance artifact.
  \item \textbf{Boundary conditions}
    (Sections~\ref{sec:subspace},~\ref{sec:scale}). An induction-subspace probe
    returns a bounded optimizer-invariant null (Appendix, with an explicit
    mapping-validity caveat). A 24-configuration scale grid
    ($L\in\{16,24,32\}\times d\in\{256,512\}\times\text{depth}\in\{2,4\}$, both
    optimizers, 96 runs) shows the staircase order surviving scale (positive rank
    index 24/24, AdamW/Muon sign agreement 12/12), with $L=24$ a high-variance
    bimodal pocket reported as a bounded robustness check, not a scaling law.
  \item \textbf{Theory} (Section~\ref{sec:arch}). Proposition~D1 (depth as
    interaction-order efficiency separation) and Proposition~G1 (Walsh-basis
    gradient correlation) supply efficiency arguments, not expressivity
    impossibility theorems, for the depth threshold and the optimizer-invariant
    order.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research questions and pre-specified analyses.}
We state four questions and the analyses that answer them (analysis plan, not a
prospectively registered deposit; Methods detail estimators and gates; Results report
them in this order of scientific priority):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] Is training-necessity of the highest-degree component (freeze)
    optimizer-dependent? \emph{Analysis:} attention vs.\ MLP freeze arms under AdamW,
    Muon, and SGDM at matched architecture, with a scoped scale audit at
    $L{=}24$, $d{=}256$.
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] Is the \emph{trained} degree-4 computation localized to the same
    sub-network across optimizers (ablation)? \emph{Analysis:} trained-model mean-ablation
    selectivity with seed-level bootstrap and a paired within-seed
    MLP-minus-attention contrast.
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] Is the low-to-high acquisition order optimizer-invariant?
    \emph{Primary analysis:} pre-specified TOST on SI across AdamW/Muon/SGDM at SESOI
    $\Delta=1.5$ (report uncorrected and Holm-corrected $p$-values). \emph{Sensitivity:}
    smallest multiplicity-corrected margin at which all pairs pass is $\pm2.0$ SI
    (Holm $p\le0.006$); not a second pre-specified primary. \emph{Robustness:} one-sample
    tests against $\mathrm{SI}=1$; depth$\geq2$; target-family/task-instance draws;
    24-configuration scale grid (not a scaling law).
  \item[\textbf{RQ4}] Does the optimizer reorganize geometry or only timing?
    \emph{Analysis:} matched-geometry emergence-step ratios across optimizers; induction
    subspace capture vs.\ a random baseline (bounded null).
\end{enumerate}
We report the analysis-plan TOST hierarchy as primary; secondary and post-hoc checks
(e.g., one-sample $t$ vs.\ $\mathrm{SI}=1$, SESOI sweep, SI-composition sensitivity)
are labeled as such. No external registry deposit is claimed.

Section~\ref{sec:arch} makes the theoretical logic behind our two main geometry results
explicit: Proposition~D1 (depth as interaction-order efficiency
separation) explains the depth threshold via a composition argument on cross-position
products; Proposition~G1 (Walsh-basis gradient correlation) explains the
low-to-high acquisition order as reflecting a target-structure constraint that
preconditioners cannot override. Both propositions are efficiency arguments, not expressivity impossibility
theorems, and their scope is stated explicitly in context.
The published literature establishes that feature acquisition is sequential and
low-dimensional (Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}; Table~\ref{tab:acq-landscape} lists the
underlying studies), but leaves open whether an orthogonalizing
optimizer can override that order, which sub-network owns the highest-degree step, and how
tight the emergence subspace is---the questions this paper addresses with scoped,
multi-seed probes.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:setup}

We use two controlled emergence tasks on a two-layer transformer (${\sim}397$k
parameters for the degree-staircase scalar-readout variant; ${\sim}427$k parameters for
the in-context induction causal language-model (LM) variant). The
first is a Boolean degree-staircase target $g = \sum_k \chi_{S_k}$, a sum of one
monomial per degree $k = 1..4$ over $L = 16$ input bits $x\in\{\pm1\}^{L}$, where
$\chi_{S}(x)=\prod_{i\in S}x_i$; trained online, per-degree Walsh
correlations track which degree is acquired when. A degree's half-time $t_k$ is the step at
which its Walsh correlation first reaches half of its final value
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:halftime}), and the staircase index
(SI; Eq.~\eqref{eq:si}; $\mathrm{SI} = 1$ means simultaneous
learning, $\mathrm{SI} \gg 1$ a sharp staircase) summarizes the ordering. The second is an
in-context induction/repeat stream; we record the emergence step, its cross-seed
variance, and the effective dimension of the cumulative parameter-update ($\Delta\theta$)
via principal-component analysis (PCA), together with the fraction of update variance captured by the idealized
3-parameter ansatz directions (definitions below). Optimizers are AdamW, Muon, and SGDM; architectural
manipulations are freeze arms (attention vs.\ the multilayer perceptron, MLP), width $\{64..512\}$, and depth
$\{1, 2, 4\}$.

\paragraph{Ordering estimators and fit gate.} All ordering quantities are computed from
the per-evaluation Walsh-correlation log of each run (one evaluation every $50$ steps on a
fixed held-out set of $8192$ inputs). Let $f_t$ denote the model's scalar output at step
$t$ (the difference of the two class logits at the readout position), $\rho_k(t)$ the
Pearson correlation between $f_t$ and the degree-$k$ monomial $\chi_{S_k}$ over the
held-out set, and $T$ the final step. The fit correlation is the Pearson correlation
between $f_t$ and the full target $g$ on the same held-out set. The half-time of degree
$k$ is the first evaluation step at which the degree reaches half of its own final level,
\begin{equation}
  t_k \;=\; \min\bigl\{\,t \;:\; |\rho_k(t)| \geq \tfrac{1}{2}\,|\rho_k(T)|\,\bigr\},
  \label{eq:halftime}
\end{equation}
defined only when $\tfrac12 |\rho_k(T)| > 10^{-6}$: a degree that is never learned has an
undefined (censored) half-time rather than a spurious one, and where a near-zero final
level makes $t_k$ formally defined but meaningless (a noise crossing at the first
evaluation), we say so in context (the ``failed-to-learn floor''). The staircase index of
a run is the geometric mean of adjacent half-time ratios over the consecutively defined
degrees $k_1 < \dots < k_{m+1}$ with $t_{k_j} > 0$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SI}
  \;=\;
  \Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{m} t_{k_{j+1}}/t_{k_j}\Bigr)^{1/m}.
  \label{eq:si}
\end{equation}
The number of ratios $m$ therefore varies by run, and we report its distribution
rather than leave it implicit. Across the 45 staircase runs $m=3$ in 22 (AdamW 8,
Muon 7, SGDM 7), $m=2$ in 21 (6, 8, 7) and $m=1$ in 2 (one AdamW, one SGDM). A degree
leaves the product when $t_k=0$, that is when its correlation at initialization already
exceeds half its final value; no half-time is formally undefined anywhere in this
dataset. This removes degree 1 in 15 of the 45 runs (5, 6 and 4 for AdamW, Muon and
SGDM) and degree 4 in 10 (3, 2 and 5). The two cases have different causes. Degree 1 is
an initialization lottery, since an untrained model occasionally starts with a
degree-1 correlation of $0.25$--$0.41$ against a final value near $0.55$, and because
seeds are shared across optimizers the affected runs largely coincide. Degree 4 leaves
the product when it is never acquired, so its final level sits at the noise floor and
half of it falls below the initialization value. The staircase rank
index of a run is the Spearman rank correlation
$r = \rho_{\mathrm{S}}\bigl((1,2,3,4),\,(t_1,\dots,t_4)\bigr) \in [-1,+1]$ ($+1$:
half-times ascend with degree, the target's low-to-high order fully recovered; $0$: no
ordering; negative: inverted), computed only for seeds whose four half-times are all
defined; the rank index of a cell is the mean of $r$ over its included seeds. Because
half-times are quantized to the $50$-step evaluation grid, ties occur (two degrees
crossing within one evaluation interval). The $d=128$ analyses assign tied half-times
distinct ordinal ranks (tie order left to the sort), while the scale-grid and $L=24$,
$d=256$ freeze-audit analyses use average (mid-)ranks; the two conventions coincide
without ties, and recomputing every reported cell under both changes no sign and no
conclusion (largest cell-level shift $0.26$, on the depth-1 control where degrees~3--4
are never learned and ties dominate; $0.17$ elsewhere). Finally, ordering comparisons are
meaningful only for runs that fit the target, so cross-optimizer ordering comparisons
exclude cells whose median final fit correlation is below $0.85$ (marked fit-gated where
they occur). The gate value is anchored to the target's structure: the four components
have equal weight, so a model that has fitted three of the four attains fit
$\sqrt{3/4}\approx 0.866$, and $0.85$ sits just below this three-component plateau,
excluding only cells that failed to fit even the low-degree scaffold. For binary
acquisition tallies (tables, Fisher exact tests, and onset panels), a seed
\emph{acquires} degree~$k$ when its final $|\rho_k(T)|\ge 0.3$; this is the primary
threshold used throughout unless noted. The layer-resolved Muon localization
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ownershiploc}) additionally marks residual acquisition above a looser
$0.15$ floor, chosen only to sit above the both-frozen control ($+0.002$) rather than as
a second primary criterion. Because the
per-seed degree-4 correlation is bimodal in the freeze arms (seeds either acquire the
component or fail near zero), wherever a mean-based Welch test carries a freeze-contrast
claim we report a two-sided Fisher exact test on the corresponding
acquisition/fail-count $2\times2$ table alongside it.

\paragraph{Induction estimators (emergence step and update subspace).}
On the induction/repeat stream the per-evaluation in-context-learning (ICL) score is
$\mathrm{ICL}=\mathrm{acc}_{\mathrm{repeat}}-\mathrm{acc}_{\mathrm{first}}$ (next-token
accuracy on repeat positions minus first-occurrence positions). The emergence step is the
first evaluation step at which $\mathrm{ICL}\ge 0.5$ (or undefined if the run never
crosses). The update-subspace audit forms the cumulative parameter update
$\Delta\theta$ over training and reports (i)~the captured variance fraction
$\lVert P_{\mathcal{A}}\Delta\theta\rVert^{2}/\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert^{2}$, the squared
energy of $\Delta\theta$ in the span of three idealized induction-circuit ansatz
directions $\mathcal{A}=\{D_1,D_2,D_3\}$ (prev-token query-key (QK), match QK, output-value (OV) copy; mapping
caveat in Appendix~\ref{sec:subspace}), and (ii)~the PCA effective dimension: the number
of principal components of the update trajectory needed for $90\%$ explained variance.
The dashed ``random baseline'' $0.0061$ in Figs.~\ref{fig:dissoc}--\ref{fig:subspace} is
the captured fraction of a synthetic random $\Delta$ under the same projector
(self-test), not an analytic degrees-of-freedom formula.

\paragraph{Protocol and reproducibility.} Unless noted, the staircase experiments use
$d_{\mathrm{model}}=128$, two layers, four attention heads, and $L=16$ input bits, trained
online for 4000 steps; each reported arm aggregates $n=15$ seeds for the staircase-index
and AdamW freeze comparisons ($n=5$ for the Muon and SGDM ownership arms reported in
Section~\ref{sec:ownership}, $n=15$ for the Muon layer-resolved localization arms, and
$n=3$ for the Muon learning-rate sensitivity check), with seeds
varying both initialization and the online data stream so that cross-seed spread reflects
genuine run-to-run variability. The freeze arms hold the architecture and all
hyperparameters fixed and zero the gradients of the named sub-network (attention or MLP),
so any difference is attributable to that component rather than to a capacity or
optimization-budget change. Complementary trained-model mean-ablations replace a named
block's activation by its per-position held-out mean at fixed checkpoints
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). Target-family robustness replaces the equal-weight ladder by
$g=\sum_k a_k\chi_{S_k}$ with geometric decay $a_k=2^{-k}$, inverse weights $a_k=2^{k}$,
or a gapped equal-weight support on degrees $\{1,3,4\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:arch}). The induction experiments use two grids. The subspace audit sweeps context length
$L\in\{64,96,128,192,256\}$ at 9 seeds per cell (45 AdamW runs), augmented by an 18-run
idealization-hardening arm ($L\in\{64,128,256\}$, two batch/learning-rate settings, 3 seeds;
AdamW), giving the 63 runs reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:subspace}. The timing
comparison uses a separate three-optimizer grid at $L\in\{64,128,256\}$ with 10 seeds
per cell and evaluation every 100 steps, refined by a fine-grained arm
(evaluation every 10 steps, 5 seeds) where the 100-step cadence floors the
measurement (Section~\ref{sec:timing}).
All quantities are computed from the
same per-step Walsh-correlation and parameter-update logs used to produce the figures;
code, logs, and figure artifacts are openly archived (see Data availability). Figure~\ref{fig:scheme} summarizes the dissociation: freeze$\perp$ablation (ownership) on a geometry$\perp$timing substrate (architecture sets order; optimizer sets the clock).

\paragraph{Statistical analysis plan (summary).}
Geometry claims rest on the pre-specified Welch TOST of SI at SESOI $\Delta=1.5$ SI units
(Section~\ref{sec:si}), with Holm correction across the three optimizer pairs; freeze and
ablation claims report per-arm acquisition rates, medians, and (where means are used)
Fisher exact tests on acquisition/fail tables alongside Welch contrasts. Multiplicity:
Holm correction across the three optimizer pairwise TOST tests for the primary
equivalence claim. Fit gate, half-time censoring, SI composition ($m$), the
$|\rho_k|\ge 0.3$ acquisition threshold, and the ICL emergence threshold $0.5$ are defined
above and applied uniformly. Sample sizes are fixed by design ($n=15$ primary staircase
and AdamW freeze; $n=8$ ablation and large-scale freeze audit; smaller $n$ for scoped
Muon/SGDM ownership arms) and are not increased after interim peeks.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

We report results in scientific-priority order matching RQ1--RQ4: the two ownership
probes first (Sections~\ref{sec:ownership}--\ref{sec:ablation}), then the
optimizer-invariant acquisition geometry they act on
(Sections~\ref{sec:si}--\ref{sec:arch}), then timing and scale robustness
(Sections~\ref{sec:timing}--\ref{sec:scale}), and a synthesis
(Section~\ref{sec:synthesis}).


\subsection{Component ownership is optimizer- and configuration-dependent}\label{sec:ownership}

We ask first which sub-network must be \emph{trained} for the highest-degree component
to be acquired; the acquisition-order geometry that component participates in is
established in Section~\ref{sec:si}. At identical architecture the necessity structure of
the highest-degree component depends on the optimizer: training attention is necessary
for degree~4 under AdamW, while under Muon either sub-network suffices at $d=128$,
$L=16$: freezing attention or the MLP alike leaves degree-4 acquisition intact
(Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt}), a two-path redundancy we localize to layer~1 below.
Only the training-necessity structure is optimizer-dependent, and, as we show below, the
Muon pattern is itself config-dependent (the attention bypass at $d=128$ reverses to an
attention rescue at $L=24$, $d=256$). Section~\ref{sec:ablation} complements these
training-time freeze probes with trained-model ablations, which answer the different
question of where the acquired computation resides (point-estimate agreement across
optimizers; interval support scoped as in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Scope of the freeze probe.} The freeze arms zero the gradients of a
sub-network at its random initialization, so they measure whether training that
component is necessary for degree-4 acquisition, not whether the component participates
in the learned computation: attention with frozen random weights can retain substantial
function \citep{dong2025randomattention}, and a freeze also changes the optimization
problem's initial conditions, which the Muon reversal below shows can matter as much as
the architecture (the same freeze that is inert at $d=128$ rescues acquisition at
$L=24$, $d=256$). Throughout, ``necessary'' therefore means necessary to train at the
stated configuration. Table~\ref{tab:freeze-scope} summarizes every freeze outcome with
its scoped reading before the detailed evidence.

For the AdamW leg we take the degree-4 correlation at $n = 15$ (seed count increased
from $n = 5$) as the primary evidence. The overall staircase-fit gap between attention-freeze
and the unfrozen baseline is no longer significant at $n = 15$ (attn $0.841 \pm 0.076$ vs none
$0.885 \pm 0.080$; difference $+0.044$, Welch $t = 1.49$, 95\% CI $[-0.014, +0.102]$
includes 0; the $n = 5$ ``$0.80$ vs $0.92$, $p \approx 0.027$'' was a small-sample fluke
and is dropped). The robust signal is high-degree acquisition: the attention-frozen model
fails degree 4 (deg-4 final correlation, means over $n=15$: attn $+0.035$ vs none $+0.214$ / MLP $+0.309$;
none-vs-attn gap $+0.180$, Welch $t = 2.98$, 95\% CI $[+0.055, +0.304]$ excludes 0;
leave-one-out $|t|$ $2.67$--$4.16$, all $> 2$; 14/15 attention-frozen seeds fail degree 4
(final correlation $<0.1$), versus 6/15 unfrozen and 5/15 MLP-frozen). Because a mean
test on a bimodal per-seed distribution can mislead, we also report Fisher exact tests
on these fractions: the attention-freeze fail excess is significant (14/15 vs.\ 6/15,
two-sided $p=0.005$) while the MLP arm is not distinguishable from baseline (5/15 vs.\
6/15, $p=1.0$); on the stricter acquisition criterion ($\geq0.3$,
Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt}) the same contrast is 1/15 vs.\ 6/15 ($p=0.080$). The per-seed
degree-4 distribution is bimodal, with seeds either acquiring the component
(${\approx}+0.43$ to $+0.52$) or failing near $0$, so Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt}
reports medians and acquisition fractions alongside these means (medians: attn $-0.000$,
none $+0.170$, MLP $+0.461$; the none-vs-attn contrast is $-0.180$ as a difference of
means and $-0.170$ as a difference of medians, and we label which is quoted at each
use). The degree-4 half-time is 0 because degree 4
is never learned, a failed-to-learn artifact, not ``learned instantly.'' MLP-freeze
preserves or enhances degree 4 ($0.309$). So the asymmetry holds robustly at $n = 15$:
training attention, not the MLP, is necessary for degree-4 \emph{acquisition} under
AdamW at this configuration---a training-necessity claim, not a claim that the finished
computation is deposited in attention (see Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); the overall-fit
gap and the rank-index inversion are washed-out / noisy corroborators only.

The ownership pattern is confirmed by re-running the freeze grid under Muon
and SGDM: freezing attention does not block degree 4 under Muon ($\Delta_{\mathrm{attn}}
= +0.005$; Muon reaches degree 4 $\approx 0.50$ and fit $1.0$ in every freeze arm),
and SGDM keeps median degree~4 near the floor in all arms (fit-gated; sparse seed-level acquisition only, Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt}). The attention bottleneck is therefore a property of
the AdamW optimization regime; under Muon's orthogonalized updates the MLP-only
sub-network already suffices at $d=128$, and this section's ownership claim is explicitly scoped to
AdamW, not an optimizer-invariant architectural fact
(Figs.~\ref{fig:arch}, \ref{fig:ownershipopt}, and~\ref{fig:ownershiplr};
Tables~\ref{tab:ownershipopt} and~\ref{tab:ownershiplr}). A fresh Muon learning-rate
sweep confirms that this bypass is not a single-lr artifact: at lr $0.01$ and $0.02$ the
attention-minus-unfrozen degree-4 gap is near zero ($-0.008$ and $+0.003$), while at lr
$0.04$ the unfrozen arm is less stable but the attention-frozen arm still reaches median
degree-4 correlation $0.511$.

\paragraph{The AdamW attention-necessity result tightens at larger scale.} A fresh $n=8$
freeze grid at $L=24$, $d=256$ replicates and sharpens the AdamW ownership claim
(Table~\ref{tab:scale-freeze-adamw}): the unfrozen and MLP-frozen arms leave degree 4
partially learned ($0.234\pm0.229$ and $0.226\pm0.221$, 4/8 seeds failing each), while
attention-freeze drives it to the floor in every seed ($-0.002\pm0.009$, 8/8 fail). The
attention effect is now $\Delta_{\mathrm{attn}}=-0.237$, 95\% CI $[-0.397,-0.084]$
(excludes 0), Cohen's $d=-1.37$, $p=0.029$, while the MLP effect stays at 0 (CI includes
0), cleaner than the original $d=128$ estimate ($\Delta_{\mathrm{attn}}=-0.180$, a
difference of arm means; the difference of medians in Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt} is
$-0.170$; 14/15 fail): the larger-scale replication strengthens, rather than qualifies,
the AdamW attention-necessity claim. A Fisher exact test on the degree-4 fail fractions
(attn 8/8 vs.\ none 4/8) gives two-sided $p=0.077$: the exact-count test is underpowered
at $n=8$ per arm, so the interval evidence above carries the inference at this scale.

\paragraph{A reversal at larger scale: attention-freeze rescues, rather than bypasses,
Muon's degree-4 acquisition.} The same $L=24$, $d=256$ audit revises, rather than merely
extends, the Muon side of this section. At $d=128$, $L=16$ Muon's unfrozen baseline
reliably reaches degree 4 ($\approx 0.50$, Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt}) and freezing
attention leaves it untouched ($\Delta_{\mathrm{attn}}=+0.005$), the ``bypass'' reading.
At $L=24$, $d=256$ the unfrozen Muon baseline is itself weak and bimodal on degree 4
($0.067\pm0.160$, 7/8 seeds failing; fit $0.869\pm0.062$; rank index $+0.109$), an
instability cross-validated by the independent scale-grid cell at the same configuration

leaving it alone, reaching $0.499\pm0.010$ with 0/8 failures, fit $1.0000\pm0.0000$, and a

$[+0.308,+0.501]$, Cohen's $d=+3.58$, $p=0.0002$, and the rescue is exact-test robust

$L=16$ or $L=32$ is untested. Far from contradicting the two-path-redundancy reading
above, the reversal strengthens it: it is further evidence that which path Muon uses is a
property of the training dynamics at a given scale, not a fixed circuit.

\paragraph{Localizing the Muon two-path redundancy to layer 1.} At $d=128$, $L=16$, that
``either sub-network suffices'' under Muon is a genuine two-path property of the hidden
network, not an incompletely frozen readout, and it is localized to the second layer. A floor control that
freezes both sub-networks drives degree-4 acquisition to the floor (median final Walsh
correlation $+0.002$, below the $0.15$ acquisition threshold; $n=15$), so when a single
freeze leaves degree 4 intact the surviving path is real rather than a leaked signal through
a partly-frozen component. Resolving the freeze by layer then localizes the carrier:
freezing either layer-0 sub-network leaves degree 4 essentially unimpaired (layer-0
attention $+0.499$, layer-0 MLP $+0.469$, both $\approx$ the unfrozen level), while freezing
the layer-1 attention drives it to the floor ($+0.022$) and freezing the layer-1 MLP largely
suppresses it ($+0.183$). Under Muon the highest-degree component is therefore carried in
layer 1, with either layer-1 sub-network able to support it
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ownershiploc}).

This is a statement about the learned dynamics, not a static wiring diagram. Freezing the
full attention stack leaves degree 4 alive (median $+0.504$, $n=5$;
Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt}), yet freezing only the
layer-1 attention kills it: the two freezes disagree because they set different initial
conditions: a full-attention freeze forces an MLP route from step~0, whereas the partial
(layer-1-only) freeze lets the model first commit to an attention circuit it then cannot
complete. Which of the two redundant paths carries degree 4 is thus training-path-dependent,
so we report a verified two-path redundancy localized to layer~1 rather than a fixed
component-to-degree assignment.\footnote{A frontier-degree extension of this freeze
test, repeating the ownership ablation under a degree-5 target, was inconclusive: degree~5
was not acquired even in the unfrozen control at the $4{,}000$-step budget (median degree-5
Walsh correlation $\leq 0.01$ under both AdamW and Muon), so there was no acquired component
to ablate at the frontier.}

\subsection{Trained-model ablations rank the degree-4-selective locus at the layer-1 MLP}\label{sec:ablation}

The freeze probes of Section~\ref{sec:ownership} measure whether training a component is
necessary (the scope note above); they are silent on the complementary question of which
component carries the learned degree-4 computation in the trained network. A
trained-model ablation experiment answers that question directly. Where freeze probes
are optimizer-dependent, ablation returns a point-estimate regularity: the
degree-4-selective carrier ranks first at the layer-1 MLP under both optimizers (point
estimates $+0.37$ AdamW / $+0.34$ Muon, robust in sign, with 95\% CIs that include zero
at $n=8$), an ordering that excludes zero as a paired within-seed MLP-minus-attention
contrast only under Muon ($+0.141$, 95\% CI $[+0.058, +0.224]$; AdamW paired $+0.090$
$[-0.159, +0.315]$).
Sixteen fresh runs of the published $d=128$, two-layer configuration (8 seeds
$\times$ \{AdamW, Muon\}; the same fresh-seed bank whose unablated medians reproduce the
published cell values, Section~\ref{sec:arch}) were checkpointed at 12 steps spanning
training. At each checkpoint we mean-ablate a sub-network, replacing the output of the
named attention or MLP blocks by its per-position mean over the held-out evaluation set
(recomputed per checkpoint), and re-measure fit and per-degree Walsh correlations under
seven conditions: unablated, full attention, full MLP, and the four layer-resolved arms
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).

Two arms are structurally degenerate by the architecture's wiring and are reported as a
design note rather than as evidence about the trained computation: input reaches the
readout position only through the attention pathway, in practice through layer-0
attention. Full-attention ablation accordingly returns identically zero fit and
per-degree correlations at every checkpoint in all 16 models, and layer-0-attention
ablation returns fit near zero (largest $|$fit$|$ $0.32$, driven by a weakly inverted
degree-1 response in 2/8 seeds per optimizer). The informative attention arm is
therefore layer 1, and the MLP arms are unaffected by this degeneracy.

Both informative results agree in direction under AdamW and Muon (with the interval
caveats below). First, ablating trained layer-1 attention
degrades degrees 2--4 collectively, gradedly under Muon and near-uniformly under AdamW,
rather than degree-4-selectively: under AdamW the median per-degree correlations fall
from $0.534/0.512/0.492$ (degrees 2/3/4, unablated) to $0.016/0.006/0.003$, and under
Muon from $0.516/0.519/0.361$ to $0.345/0.018/0.004$, while degree 1 survives (medians
$0.917$ and $0.717$). Second, the degree-4-selective carrier localizes to the layer-1
MLP: ablating it leaves degrees 1--3 largely intact (AdamW $0.530/0.484/0.363$; Muon
$0.587/0.507/0.436$) while removing degree 4 ($0.492\to0.059$ under AdamW,
$0.361\to0.013$ under Muon). Defining selectivity as the degree-4 median drop minus the
mean degree-1--3 median drop, the layer-1 MLP arm scores $+0.369$ under AdamW and
$+0.344$ under Muon, the largest selectivity of any arm under both optimizers and
differing by only $0.025$ in point estimate; every attention arm's selectivity is smaller
(full attention $-0.030$/$-0.153$, layer-1 attention $+0.279$/$+0.204$). A seed-level
percentile bootstrap (resampling the 8 seeds with replacement, $20{,}000$ resamples,
recomputing the median-based selectivity per resample) quantifies the uncertainty of
this scalar: the layer-1 MLP selectivity is $+0.369$, 95\% CI $[-0.045, +0.415]$ under
AdamW and $+0.344$ $[-0.039, +0.525]$ under Muon; the intervals reach marginally below
zero because the seed bank's degree-4 acquisition is itself bimodal, so resampled
medians occasionally land on non-acquiring seed subsets. The paired MLP-minus-attention
(layer-1) selectivity difference excludes zero under Muon ($+0.141$, 95\% CI
$[+0.058, +0.224]$) but not under AdamW ($+0.090$ $[-0.159, +0.315]$): the
MLP-over-attention selectivity ordering is interval-supported under Muon and a
point-estimate regularity under AdamW.

The two probes therefore dissociate cleanly. Freezing, a training-necessity probe, is
optimizer-dependent: attention must be trained under AdamW at this configuration but not
under Muon (Table~\ref{tab:ownershipopt}); SGDM is uninformative on freeze. Ablation, a
trained-computation probe, is a point-estimate regularity: in the trained model the
degree-4-selective locus ranks first at the layer-1 MLP under both optimizers (absolute
CIs include zero at $n{=}8$; interval support for MLP$>$attn only under Muon), and trained
attention supports degrees 2--4 collectively rather than degree 4 specifically. The disagreement is not a contradiction but the content of
the scope note in Section~\ref{sec:ownership}: a freeze tests whether training a
component lies on the causal path to acquiring degree 4, an ablation tests where the
acquired computation resides. Read jointly: under AdamW the attention pathway must be
trained for degree 4 to be acquired at all, yet the degree-4-selective component of the
finished function is deposited in the layer-1 MLP, the same layer-1 locus the Muon freeze
localization identified (freezing Muon's layer-1 MLP suppresses degree 4 to $+0.183$,
Section~\ref{sec:ownership}). The ownership claim is accordingly sharpened into a
two-probe statement: which sub-network must be trained to acquire the top degree
is optimizer-dependent; where the degree-4-selective computation ranks in the trained
model is a point-estimate regularity (absolute CIs include zero at $n{=}8$; paired
MLP$>$attn interval support only under Muon).

\subsection{Geometry: the staircase survives orthogonalization, and the three optimizers agree}\label{sec:si}

Sections~\ref{sec:ownership} and~\ref{sec:ablation} localized \emph{where} the degree-4
computation lives; this section establishes the \emph{order} in which the degrees are
acquired, and shows the optimizer does not set it.

Muon does not flatten the degree staircase. At $n = 15$ per optimizer the staircase index
SI (Eq.~\eqref{eq:si}) is $2.98 \pm 1.39$ (AdamW), $3.53 \pm 1.54$ (Muon) and $3.20 \pm 1.61$ (SGDM). The
pre-specified analysis of these values asks whether the optimizers agree, not whether they
differ, so it is a two-one-sided equivalence test
\citep{schuirmann1987tost,lakens2017equivalence}
(TOST, Welch; null $|\mu_i-\mu_j|\ge\Delta$ rejected only if both one-sided tests pass;
analysis-plan SESOI $\Delta = 1.5$ SI units, chosen \emph{a priori} so that
a between-optimizer difference smaller than $1.5$ still leaves both optimizers on the
staircase side of the competing simultaneous-learning / spectral-flattening prediction
$\mathrm{SI}\approx 1$ \citep{dragutinovic2026usemuon}, rather than near the flat-learning
null) concludes equivalence for all three pairs at uncorrected
$\alpha=0.05$: for Muon against AdamW the difference is $+0.55$ SI, 90\% CI
$[-0.36, +1.46] \subset (-1.5, +1.5)$, $p_{\mathrm{TOST}} = 0.044$, and the SGDM pairs
give $p_{\mathrm{TOST}} = 0.014$ (vs.\ AdamW) and $0.026$ (vs.\ Muon) at the same margin
(Table~\ref{tab:tost-si}). The three tests form a family, and under Holm multiplicity
correction the $\pm1.5$ verdict holds for AdamW vs.\ SGDM only, while equivalence within
a $\pm2.0$ margin survives the corrected level for all three pairs
($p \leq 0.006$). We therefore treat the analysis-plan $\pm1.5$ margin as primary
(uncorrected pass; Holm partial) and $\pm2.0$ as a multiplicity-safe \emph{sensitivity}
bound (smallest corrected margin at which all pairs pass), with the full correction and
margin analysis below. An independent $4\times$
Muon-learning-rate sweep holds SI in $2.36$--$3.26$, never moving toward
$\mathrm{SI} \approx 1$, so the predicted flattening is absent across seeds and learning
rate.

The equivalence test compares the optimizers with each other and does not directly test
the value the competing account predicts, so we add a complementary point-null check
against that value. A one-sample $t$-test against $\mathrm{SI} = 1$ rejects for
all three: $t(14) = 5.50$, $6.34$ and $5.28$ respectively, with $p = 7.9\times10^{-5}$,
$1.8\times10^{-5}$ and $1.2\times10^{-4}$. All three survive Holm correction over the
family of three tests with more than two orders of magnitude to spare, the $95\%$
confidence intervals on the mean ($[2.21, 3.75]$, $[2.67, 4.38]$, $[2.30, 4.09]$) place
the lower endpoint at more than twice the predicted value, and only 2 of the 45 runs fall
below $\mathrm{SI} = 1$ at all. Because SI is a ratio statistic we also ran the
nonparametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test, which agrees ($p = 1.8\times10^{-4}$,
$6.1\times10^{-5}$, $1.2\times10^{-4}$); Shapiro--Wilk does not reject normality for any
optimizer ($p = 0.49$, $0.12$, $0.058$), so we lead with the $t$-test and treat the
Wilcoxon as confirmation. The inferential hierarchy is explicit: the pre-specified
analysis is the equivalence test of the preceding paragraph, and these one-sample tests
are a complementary point-null check reported for completeness, ruling out the value
$\mathrm{SI} = 1$ that a TOST does not itself address. The point-null result has the
further property of not depending on any between-optimizer comparison.

\paragraph{Multiplicity and margin sensitivity.} The corrected verdicts summarized above
are reported in full here (Table~\ref{tab:tost-sens}). Under Holm correction
at family level $\alpha = 0.05$, only AdamW vs.\ SGDM remains equivalent at the $\pm1.5$
margin ($p = 0.014 \leq 0.017$); Muon vs.\ SGDM ($p = 0.026$ against threshold $0.025$)
and Muon vs.\ AdamW ($p = 0.044$) do not, and the Bonferroni-level TOST interval for Muon
vs.\ AdamW ($96.7\%$ CI $[-0.65, +1.75]$) exceeds the margin. Equivalence at the
pre-specified $\pm1.5$ margin is therefore a sensitivity result for the Muon pairs,
holding at the uncorrected level only. A SESOI
sensitivity sweep locates the decision boundary: at margin $\pm1.0$ SI no pair is
equivalent even uncorrected ($p = 0.08$--$0.20$), at $\pm1.5$ all three pairs pass
uncorrected, and at $\pm2.0$ all three pass at the Bonferroni-corrected level
($p \leq 0.006$), which is the margin at which we state the headline equivalence. Since a pre-specified margin that sits close to the smallest margin the
data would support invites the objection that it was chosen after the fact, we state that
minimum: writing it as $|\hat\Delta| + t_{1-\alpha,\nu}\,\mathrm{SE}$ (standard error) and taking the
worst pair, the family becomes equivalent at any margin above $1.464$ uncorrected and
above $1.752$ under Bonferroni. The pre-specified $1.5$ therefore clears the uncorrected
requirement by $0.036$ units and falls $0.252$ units short of the corrected one, which is
the same marginality the corrected $p$-values report, seen on the margin scale. The corrected-level
conclusion is thus equivalence within $\pm2$ SI units for all three pairs, with every
point estimate sitting at $3.0$--$3.5$, two units above the flattening prediction, so
flattening to $\mathrm{SI} \approx 1$ is ruled out decisively; the sharper
claim of a between-optimizer difference below $1.5$ SI units is supported at the
uncorrected level only, and we scope every downstream use of the equivalence accordingly.

On the stricter multiplicative scale, a log-SI TOST
(factor-$1.5$ margin) is inconclusive (Muon's SI is ${\sim}24\%$ higher as a point
estimate, with the 90\% CI reaching a factor ${\sim}1.7$), so we claim equivalence on the
absolute-SI margin and report the log-scale test as inconclusive rather
than overclaiming a factor-level invariance. Orthogonalization (spectral flattening) thus
shows no sign of extrapolating from deep-linear singular modes to function-space Fourier
degrees (Fig.~\ref{fig:si}).

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the composition of the SI estimator.} Because $m$ varies by run
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}), we report two sensitivity analyses alongside the primary
full-sample result. The first is a complete-case analysis restricted to the 22 runs in
which all four degrees have positive half-times (AdamW 8, Muon 7, SGDM 7). It narrows the
Muon--AdamW difference from $+0.55$ to $+0.14$ and lowers $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}$ from
$0.044$ to $0.0144$, which would clear the Holm threshold of $0.0167$ and so would
upgrade the Muon--AdamW pair at the $\pm1.5$ margin from uncorrected-only to
corrected-level equivalent. We do not use it
that way. Complete-case membership is conditioned on degree 4 having a positive
half-time, which is itself an outcome: degree 4 is never acquired in 7 of 15 AdamW runs
against 2 of 15 for Muon (Fisher $p = 0.11$), so the subset is not missing at random with
respect to the quantity being compared. The exclusion is also milder than that gap
suggests, since degree 4 actually leaves the SI product in 3 AdamW and 2 Muon runs
(Fisher $p = 1.00$), and under the same restriction the other two pairs move the opposite
way ($p = 0.037$ for AdamW vs.\ SGDM and $0.039$ for Muon vs.\ SGDM, neither clearing
Holm). The refutation of $\mathrm{SI} = 1$ holds within this subset as well
($t = 4.23$, $6.10$, $3.45$; $p = 3.9\times10^{-3}$, $8.8\times10^{-4}$,
$1.4\times10^{-2}$). The second analysis recomputes SI over degrees that were actually
acquired, dropping any degree whose final $|\rho_k|$ is below $0.1$, which removes the
runs where an unacquired degree entered the product with a half-time set by noise (4 of
15 AdamW runs, 6 of 15 SGDM, none for Muon). This raises the point estimates to $4.02$
(AdamW), $3.53$ (Muon) and $4.18$ (SGDM) and strengthens the refutation
($t = 7.04$, $6.34$, $8.00$; $p \leq 1.8\times10^{-5}$), but it reverses the sign of the
Muon--AdamW difference to $-0.49$ and leaves Muon vs.\ SGDM outside the $1.5$-unit margin
($p = 0.073$; family minimum margin $1.615$). The pattern across all three definitions is
consistent: the refutation of the flattening prediction is insensitive to how partially
acquired degrees are handled, while the between-optimizer comparison is sensitive to it
and has no stable sign. This is why we rest the geometry claim on the former and scope
the latter as marginal.

\paragraph{A degree-5 extension and an isolated-parity control.} Two anchor cells test
whether the ordering geometry is a $D=4$ accident. Extending the target to a degree-5
staircase (one monomial per degree $1$--$5$, same $L=16$ testbed, 5 seeds per optimizer,
4{,}000-step budget), all three optimizers reproduce the same acquisition geometry one
degree up: degrees $1$--$3$ are learned to the familiar per-degree level (medians
$0.47$--$0.62$) while degrees $4$ and $5$ remain at the frontier (medians $\leq0.15$), so
the overall fit sits at the three-of-five-components value $\sqrt{3/5}\approx0.775$
(measured medians $0.740$--$0.780$). The frontier's location is set by the budget, but
the acquisition order is again optimizer-invariant, and now also target-depth-invariant.
An isolated degree-5 parity (the same top monomial without its staircase, the
sparse-parity setting of \citealp{barak2022hidden}) behaves qualitatively differently:
acquisition is all-or-nothing and run-to-run bistable, with $3/5$ seeds learning it
outright under Muon (fits $0.80$--$1.00$) and under AdamW ($0.74$--$0.90$) while the
remaining seeds stay at zero, and SGDM never learns it ($0/5$). The contrast cuts both
ways: inside the staircase the budget is spent on the low-degree components first and
degree~5 is starved, while in isolation the same monomial is sometimes learned
whole, direct evidence that staircase acquisition is sequential rather than the top
degree being unlearnable, consistent with the leap/sequential-learning picture
\citep{abbe2023leap}.

\subsection{Geometry: depth \texorpdfstring{$\geq 2$}{>= 2} sets the high-degree staircase step}\label{sec:arch}

The staircase is width-flat but depth-thresholded. The staircase rank index (defined
formally in Section~\ref{sec:setup}: per-seed Spearman correlation between degree and
half-time, averaged over seeds; $+1$ = the low-to-high ordering is fully recovered, $0$ =
no ordering, negative = inverted) holds at ${\sim}0.5$--$1.0$ across $d = 64$--$512$ with
no trend. A single layer fails to learn the high degrees
(degree-3/4 final correlation $\approx 0$, fit $0.71$), while two layers learn them (fit
$0.91$). This is a capacity threshold, not a ``learned-immediately'' ordering; the
single-layer degree-4 half-time of $50$ is the failed-to-learn floor, not instant
acquisition.

\paragraph{Proposition D1 (depth as interaction-order efficiency separation).}
The depth threshold relates to a known interaction-order argument in approximation theory.
The target is a sum of degree-$k$ Walsh monomials, $k$-wise multiplicative interactions
across $k$ distinct positions of the Boolean input
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:target}),
\begin{equation}
  g(x) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{4} \chi_{S_k}(x), \qquad
  \chi_{S}(x) \;=\; \prod_{i\in S} x_i, \quad x\in\{\pm1\}^{L},
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
so the degree-4 term $\chi_{S_4}=\prod_{i\in S_4}x_i$ is a four-way product.
A single transformer block provides one cross-position mixing stage (via self-attention)
followed by position-wise nonlinearity, realizing interactions of order $\lesssim 2$
efficiently; composing stages combines sub-products to raise the achievable interaction
order, so reaching a four-way product efficiently calls for depth $\geq 2$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:order}):
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\text{order} \lesssim 2}_{\text{one mixing stage}}
  \;<\; 4 \;\leq\;
  \underbrace{\text{order achievable}}_{\text{two mixing stages}} .
  \label{eq:order}
\end{equation}
This ``composition doubles achievable interaction order'' intuition is consistent with
depth-separation results for transformers \citep{sanford2023transformers}, which establish
that some sequence functions require additional depth or width that grows with the
interaction complexity.  Because softmax attention and Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU) are
universal approximators at finite width, the argument is an efficiency
separation, not a representability impossibility: a one-layer model can in principle
express degree 4 at sufficiently large $d$, but our $d = 64$--$512$ sweep is
width-flat, indicating that width alone does not resolve the gap.
The observed scale sensitivity (the two-layer recovery is a high-variance, bimodal pocket
specifically at $L=24$, while the surrounding 24-configuration scale grid shows the
staircase order present at every configuration; Section~\ref{sec:scale}) is consistent
with this reading: a constants-limited efficiency gap is expected to close gradually and
unevenly as capacity grows, rather than producing a sharp phase boundary.
\emph{Scope}: this framing applies only to the depth threshold; we explicitly do not
extend it to the component-ownership result of Section~\ref{sec:ownership}, which is AdamW-specific and is
contradicted by the Muon arm as an optimizer-invariant representability claim.

\paragraph{Proposition G1 (staircase order from Walsh-basis gradient correlation).}
The low-to-high degree acquisition order (Section~\ref{sec:si}) is accounted for by a
gradient seeding argument on the orthonormal Walsh basis.
At small initialization the population gradient of the mean squared error (MSE) loss on the degree-$k$ component
is non-negligible only once lower-degree structure has been partially fitted,
because $\chi_{S_k}$ (a $k$-wise product) is reachable in the network's function space
only after the composing sub-products exist
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:gradseed}):
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}\right|_{\deg\,k}
  \;\propto\; \big\langle \chi_{S_k},\, r_\theta \big\rangle, \qquad
  r_\theta \;=\; g - f_\theta \ \ (\text{residual}),
  \label{eq:gradseed}
\end{equation}
which stays ${\approx}0$ for high $k$ until the lower-degree residual is reduced.
This hierarchical gradient seeding (low degree seeds high degree) predicts a low-to-high
acquisition order that reflects the target's monomial structure and the
orthonormality of the Walsh basis \citep{abbe2021staircase,abbe2023leap}, not of the
optimizer's preconditioner.
A diagonal or spectral preconditioner can rescale how fast each degree advances
(timing) but cannot manufacture gradient signal along a direction where the reachable
gradient is zero.
The TOST equivalence in Section~\ref{sec:si} is precisely this prediction: the staircase
order reflects the target's basis geometry and is invariant across the optimizers tested,
while timing is optimizer-dependent.

\paragraph{Corollary 1 (timing--geometry separation).}
Under the Walsh-basis gradient seeding of Proposition~G1, let $P$ be any positive-definite
preconditioner applied to the gradient (diagonal for AdamW-type, spectral for Muon-type).
Then, under the two assumptions stated below, $P$ rescales the per-degree advancement rate
(timing) but does not alter the reachable
acquisition order, because $P$ cannot produce a nonzero update component along a Walsh
direction $\chi_{S_k}$ whose reachable gradient $\langle \chi_{S_k}, r_\theta \rangle$ is
zero. Hence acquisition order is preconditioner-invariant while acquisition time is
preconditioner-dependent; the optimizer is a clock, not a compass. Two assumptions enter:
(i)~the small-initialization regime under which the residual-seeding argument of
Proposition~G1 holds (already assumed there); and (ii)~that $P$ maps the zero-gradient
Walsh subspace to itself.
Step~(ii) is empirically supported by Section~\ref{sec:si}; a formal proof for
orthogonalized updates is left open.

\paragraph{Empirical confirmation of the bootstrap (ladder necessity).} Proposition~G1's
gradient-seeding logic predicts that a degree-$k$ component is reachable only once the
lower-degree structure has been fitted, so the nested ladder should be able to bootstrap an
optimizer into a degree it cannot reach in isolation. SGDM exhibits exactly this. On the
isolated single-degree-3 target it fails completely (final fit $\approx 0$, all five seeds;
shown later in Fig.~\ref{fig:pure3}), yet on the nested staircase it reaches a high overall fit
($0.85$) by acquiring the scaffolding degrees: degrees 1--2 are learned (final Walsh
correlation $\approx 0.59$), degree 3 is partly learned (mean $0.41$, 10/15 seeds above
$0.3$), while degree 4 remains largely unlearned (mean $0.10$, 2/15 above $0.3$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}). AdamW and Muon fit both the isolated target and the ladder. The
ladder is thus necessary for SGDM's degree-3 acquisition (the lower degrees seed
the gradient signal for the higher one, as Proposition~G1 requires), and the same
acquisition order holds across optimizers.

\paragraph{Target-family generality of the acquisition order.} Proposition~G1 attributes
the low-to-high order to the target's monomial structure, not to the particular equal-weight
staircase used above. Two alternative target families test this directly, replacing
$g=\sum_k\chi_{S_k}$ by $g=\sum_k a_k\chi_{S_k}$ (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). On the
inverse-weighted family $a_k=2^{k}$ (high-degree monomials dominate the loss), acquisition
still ascends from low to high degree (rank index $+0.61$
under AdamW, $+0.83$ under Muon; $n=15$); on the gapped equal-weight support
$\{1,3,4\}$ it likewise ascends ($+0.77$/$+0.63$); and on geometric decay $a_k=2^{-k}$ it is
noisier but non-inverting
($+0.37$/$+0.25$, the large seed spread coming from the tiny high-degree amplitudes). The
low-to-high order is thus a property of the monomial ladder rather than the equal-weight
construction, as Proposition~G1 predicts. The claim is scoped to AdamW and Muon: SGDM does
not fit the inverse-weighted target (median fit $0.11$) and is therefore fit-gated out of
its ordering comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:targetfamily}).

\paragraph{Task-instance generality of the acquisition order.} The staircase cells above
share one fixed random draw of the monomial sets (which bit subsets $S_k$ carry each
degree). To rule out a single-instance artifact, three fresh random draws of the monomial
sets (same $L=16$, one monomial per degree $1$--$4$, equal weights) were each trained
under AdamW and Muon ($n=5$ seeds per cell, 30 runs) and analyzed with exactly the
estimators and fit gate of Section~\ref{sec:setup} (ordinal ties); an accompanying $n=8$
fresh-seed re-run of the original draw under both optimizers reproduces the published
cell values through the same pipeline (median fit $0.944$/$0.954$, rank index
$+0.73$/$+0.90$ for AdamW/Muon, against $+0.67$/$+0.52$ at the original seed counts),
anchoring the comparison. All six draw$\times$optimizer cells are learnable and clear the
$0.85$ fit gate (median final fit $0.865$--$1.000$), and five of the six show a clearly
positive rank index: $+0.84\pm0.08$ (AdamW) and $+0.88\pm0.10$ (Muon) on the second draw,
$+0.96\pm0.08$ and $+0.52\pm0.59$ on the third, and $+0.72\pm0.27$ for Muon on the first.
The sixth cell (first draw, AdamW) is reported in full because it is an informative
failure mode rather than a counterexample: it clears the gate exactly at the
three-of-four-components plateau (median fit $0.865$; per-seed fits
$0.704/0.865/0.865/0.865/1.000$), because degree 4 is acquired within the $4{,}000$-step
budget in only $1/5$ seeds on this draw (Muon: $2/5$), and the censored-at-noise degree-4
half-times of the non-acquiring seeds drag the all-seed rank index to $+0.04\pm0.61$.
Where the ordering is adjudicable it is preserved: the one acquiring AdamW seed shows the
full ascending staircase (half-times $50/150/250/650$, rank $+1.0$), and restricted to
degree-4-acquiring seeds every one of the six cells has rank index $+0.84$ to $+1.00$.
The matched-fit comparison makes the geometry--timing reading explicit on fresh
instances: at each draw's AdamW-median target fit, the pointwise per-degree correlation
vectors of AdamW and Muon agree to within $0.04$ per degree, including degree 4 on the
hard draw, where both sit at ${\approx}0$, while the median step to reach that fit
differs by $3.3$--$5.7\times$ (AdamW $2{,}950$--$4{,}000$ vs.\ Muon $700$--$1{,}050$).
Per-cell SI stays on the staircase side throughout (cell means $2.1$--$3.3$). Neither
the order nor the index is therefore a property of the particular published monomial
draw; what varies across instances is whether the top degree is reached within budget,
not the order in which degrees arrive.

\subsection{Timing: the optimizer sets when emergence happens}\label{sec:timing}

Muon advances induction-head emergence by $1.6$--$3.7\times$ over AdamW (growing with context length) and ${\sim}6.9$--$12\times$ over SGDM
(Table~\ref{tab:timing}). This optimizer-driven acceleration is the topical counterpart of
recent work on accelerating grokking \citep{park2025kagrokking}, here isolated to timing at
fixed acquisition geometry. A ``zero cross-seed variance'' signature is reported with a caveat: Muon emergence steps are $30$--$70$ and AdamW $48$--$90$ on a fine 10-step
evaluation grid; AdamW is also zero-variance at $L=128$ ($90 \pm 0$),
so zero-variance is partly grid coarseness, not a Muon-exclusive phenomenon; the
timing advance itself is robust (Fig.~\ref{fig:timing}). A matched-cadence robustness
check that evaluates all three optimizers on the same coarse 100-step grid
(Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}) confirms the optimizer
ordering (SGDM slowest at every $L$, AdamW separating from Muon at $L=256$), without
relying on different evaluation cadences for different optimizers; the matched-cadence
ratios are AdamW/Muon $1.0$/$1.0$/$2.6\times$ and SGDM/Muon $2.9$/$4.8$/$4.8\times$ at
$L=64$/$128$/$256$, floored lower bounds because Muon sits at that grid's 100-step
first-evaluation floor everywhere (Table~\ref{tab:timing} marks each cell's cadence).

Combined with Section~\ref{sec:si}, this gives the dissociation directly for the staircase
geometry: the per-degree order/difficulty geometry is optimizer-invariant at matched fit
(degrees 1--3 $\approx 0.55$, degree 4 lags, under all optimizers) while the time to reach
that geometry differs ${\sim}4.5\times$.

The cleanest isolation of the timing mechanism is on a single-degree target.
Trained directly on one degree-3 monomial (rather than the nested ladder), AdamW and Muon
both fit it to near-perfect correlation ($\approx 1.0$), but Muon acquires it in
$100 \pm 0$ steps versus AdamW's mean $660$ steps, a $6.6\times$ timing advance with the
geometry held trivially fixed (one degree, one component; Fig.~\ref{fig:pure3}). SGDM never
learns the isolated target (final fit $\approx 0$ across all five seeds), so its half-time
is a failed-to-learn floor, not a fast acquisition. This single-degree control confirms
that the optimizer moves when a component is acquired, not what is acquired.

\subsection{Scale: the staircase order survives a 24-configuration grid; \texorpdfstring{$L=24$}{L=24} remains a sensitive point}\label{sec:scale}

A 24-configuration grid crossing $L\in\{16,24,32\}$, $d\in\{256,512\}$,
depth $\in\{2,4\}$ and both optimizers (96 runs; 5 seeds per $d=256$ cell, 3 seeds per
$d=512$ cell) tests whether the acquisition order is present away from the $d=128$,
$L=16$ default. It is: the mean rank index is positive in every configuration (24/24,
range $+0.035$ to $+1.000$) and the AdamW/Muon acquisition order agrees in sign in 12/12
configurations (mean $|\Delta\text{rank}|=0.265$, signed $-0.099$, within seed noise), so
the optimizer-invariant order of Section~\ref{sec:si} is also present at $1.5$--$2\times$
this paper's default width and input length, with depth flat across the grid (mean rank
$0.460$ at depth 2 vs $0.461$ at depth 4) and width showing only a slight, likely
sub-noise decline ($0.505$ at $d=256$ vs $0.417$ at $d=512$). One point is disclosed as
sensitive rather than smoothed over: $L=24$ is the hardest and noisiest cell (mean of the
per-cell median fit $0.859$, run-level median $0.866$, $2/32$ runs solved, mean rank
$0.263$; at $n=5$ per optimizer the rank-index 95\% CIs there include 0 for both AdamW
$[-0.08,+0.88]$ and Muon $[-0.21,+0.58]$), it is non-monotone in $L$, and its per-seed fit
is bimodal, clustering at the three-of-four-components value $\sqrt{3/4}\approx0.866$ and
at $1.0$. It is the same point flagged by the small-sample two- and four-seed audits that
motivated this grid (Fig.~\ref{fig:cscale} and Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}) and by the
freeze reversal of Section~\ref{sec:ownership}, and the grid establishes it as a genuine
high-variance pocket rather than an undersampling artifact. Because the grid samples a
$1.5$--$2\times$ window in width and input length and resolves no trend in either, we
report it as a bounded robustness check on the presence of the order across scale, not as
a scaling law; the corresponding row of Table~\ref{tab:evidence-ledger} carries that scope
tag.

\subsection{Synthesis: two probes, and the geometry that lets them be compared}\label{sec:synthesis}

The paper's lead result is the two-probe ownership dissociation: training-necessity
(freezing; Section~\ref{sec:ownership}) is optimizer- and config-dependent (AdamW vs
Muon; SGDM uninformative on freeze), while the trained computation (mean-ablation;
Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) ranks the degree-4-selective carrier first at the layer-1 MLP
in point estimate under both optimizers (absolute CIs include zero at $n{=}8$; paired
interval support only under Muon). That dissociation is
readable only because the acquisition geometry underneath is optimizer-invariant: the
staircase index is equivalence-tested across optimizers (Section~\ref{sec:si}), the
depth threshold and low-to-high order are architecture- and target-owned
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}), and only emergence time moves with the optimizer
(Section~\ref{sec:timing}, $1.6$--$12\times$). At matched fit the per-degree geometry is
invariant while the time to reach it differs. The freeze leg is scoped honestly (AdamW
attention-necessity at $d=128$ tightens at $L=24$, $d=256$; Muon two-path redundancy at
$d=128$ reverses to an attention rescue at that larger audit), and the
${\sim}3$-D induction subspace (Appendix~\ref{sec:subspace}) remains a bounded
optimizer-invariant null. As measured, the claim bounds the optimizer-flattening
account (no flattening of the staircase) and the fixed-ansatz account (dimension yes,
directions not detected under any optimizer). The closing metaphor is unchanged: the
optimizer is a clock, not a compass---it sets timing, not the order of acquisition, and
not (in the ablation sense) where the finished computation is deposited.

Table~\ref{tab:evidence-ledger} summarizes these eight claims with their evidence grades and scope boundaries. Figure~\ref{fig:dissoc} collects the four dissociation measurements.
Panel~(A) shows the SI strip-chart with the TOST result; panel~(B) the per-freeze-arm

fall. In panel~(A) the per-optimizer mean SI sits well above $\mathrm{SI} = 1$ for all
three optimizers (means ${\approx}3$, with only isolated single seeds near
$\mathrm{SI} = 1$); no optimizer's distribution approaches the simultaneous-learning
($\mathrm{SI} = 1$) regime that the spectral-flattening account predicts.

\paragraph{Component attribution: freeze vs.\ ablation.}
Causal interventions during training (gradient freezes, path patching) and interventions
on trained networks (activation ablation, mean ablation) are often treated as
interchangeable readouts of ``which component does the work.'' The present results show
they need not agree: freezing measures training necessity along a path, while ablation
measures where the finished computation is deposited. We therefore cite sub-network
ablation methodology \citep{nanda2023progress} as the trained-computation probe and keep
freeze arms as a separate training-necessity probe (Section~\ref{sec:ownership}), rather
than as a single undifferentiated attribution toolkit.

\paragraph{Grokking and mechanistic interpretability.}
The grokking phenomenon, delayed generalization after prolonged overfitting on small
algorithmic datasets, was identified by \citet{power2022grokking}. Mechanistic
interpretability of grokking was pioneered by \citet{nanda2023progress}, who
reverse-engineered the Fourier-rotation algorithm learned on modular addition and identified three
training phases (memorization, circuit formation, cleanup); our work studies an analogous
phase structure on the Boolean degree-staircase. Generalization dynamics beyond standard
algorithmic tasks are surveyed in \citet{liu2023omnigrok}.

\paragraph{Emergence geometry and simplicity bias.}
\citet{rende2024distributional} establish a distributional simplicity bias: transformers
acquire low-degree token interactions first. Sections~\ref{sec:si}/\ref{sec:arch} study
the same phenomenon mechanistically; the leap-complexity account \citep{abbe2023leap} is a
distinct line.
Simplicity bias in transformers on Boolean functions has been studied from a circuit
perspective by \citet{bhattamishra2023simplicity} and characterized as a low-sensitivity
inductive bias by \citet{vasudeva2025lowsensitivity}; our staircase-equivalence result
(Section~\ref{sec:si}) shows this bias is preserved by orthogonalizing updates.
Hidden phase transitions in Boolean parity learning, where SGD makes invisible progress
until a sudden transition, are the setting for \citet{barak2022hidden}, providing a
Boolean-function-learning analogue to our staircase emergence. Our degree-staircase
extends the single-parity phase-transition picture of \citet{barak2022hidden} to a
multi-degree sequential staircase: rather than one hidden-progress episode
culminating in a single parity transition, the network undergoes a cascade of $k=1\to4$
acquisition events whose ordering is fixed by the target's monomial structure.

\paragraph{Staircase / leap complexity theory.}
The theoretical backbone for sequential degree acquisition is the staircase property of
\citet{abbe2021staircase} and the leap-complexity theory of \citet{abbe2023leap}, which
formalize how hierarchical target structure forces sequential acquisition under gradient
descent. Proposition~G1 (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) instantiates this logic for the
Walsh-basis gradient-correlation argument; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} details how our
TOST result confirms the prediction and empirically extends it to an orthogonalizing
optimizer (Muon) outside the gradient-descent family the original framework assumed.

\paragraph{Induction heads and in-context learning.}
Induction heads as the mechanistic substrate of in-context learning were identified by
\citet{olsson2022induction}, who provided six lines of evidence, including causal
intervention experiments, that induction heads are the primary source of in-context
learning in attention-only transformers. The formal theoretical account of
\citet{musat2025inductionheads} argues that induction-head
emergence is confined to $3$ of a $19$-dimensional update subspace and that the
emergence time grows quadratically in the input context length;
Appendix~\ref{sec:subspace} tests
and bounds the dimensional claim (ansatz-direction capture below baseline in 62/63 runs;
mapping-validity caveat stated), while our emergence-step measurements (Section~\ref{sec:timing})
grow monotonically with context length in the same direction, though at an
optimizer-dependent rate rather than a single quadratic exponent.

\paragraph{Transformer depth and expressivity.}
Depth-separation results for transformers are established by \citet{sanford2023transformers},
who show that some sequence functions require additional depth or width growing with
interaction complexity. Proposition~D1 (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) invokes this composition
argument: a one-mixing-stage block achieves interactions of order $\lesssim 2$, so
degree-4 products require $D\geq 2$. Our empirical depth threshold ($D\geq 2$ for degree 4,
width-flat across $d=64$--$512$) is consistent with the efficiency-separation
framing of \citet{sanford2023transformers}: the boundary is not an expressivity
impossibility (a sufficiently wide single layer can in principle represent degree 4), but
an efficiency gap that our width sweep does not close. We position Proposition~D1 as an
efficiency argument of the same family, not a new depth-separation theorem.

\paragraph{Optimizer implicit bias.}
The spectral / idealized-Muon account \citep{dragutinovic2026usemuon} predicts removal of
the sequential simplicity bias; Section~\ref{sec:si} bounds it (no flattening,
equivalence-tested). AdamW's implicit bias is an $\ell_\infty$ norm constraint
\citep{xie2024adamw}, with a large-deviation analysis characterizing SGD's implicit bias
more broadly \citep{neucom2026sgdbias}, distinct from the Nesterov-momentum / spectral
dynamics of Muon \citep{beneventano2026spectraldynamics,bernstein2024anthology}; the AdamW-specific
attention-ownership result
(Section~\ref{sec:ownership}) is consistent with these optimizers solving the task via
genuinely different internal routes while arriving at the same acquisition geometry.

\paragraph{Sub-network ablation methodology.}
The freeze-component / sub-network-ablation methodology follows
\citet{dong2025randomattention}. Grokking has also been analyzed through the lens of
norm- and stability-based generalization bounds \citep{neucom2026grokking}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Component ownership is a two-probe story: training-necessity is
optimizer-dependent; trained-computation localization is a scoped point-estimate
regularity.}
The lead claim is a dissociation between two probes of component ownership
(Sections~\ref{sec:ownership},~\ref{sec:ablation}). Training-necessity depends on the
optimizer: attention must be trained under AdamW at $d=128$ (tightening at
$L=24$, $d=256$), while under Muon a layer-1 training-time two-path redundancy leaves
degree~4 intact at $d=128$ on the same architecture---and that Muon pattern itself
reverses at the $L=24$, $d=256$ audit, where attention-freeze rescues rather than
bypasses degree-4 acquisition. Trained-model ablation shows the optimizer dependence
is of the training path, not of the finished function: the degree-4-selective carrier
ranks first at the layer-1 MLP under both AdamW and Muon (selectivity $+0.369$/$+0.344$,
robust in sign; absolute CIs include zero at $n{=}8$; paired MLP-minus-attention
support only under Muon), and ablating trained attention degrades degrees 2--4
collectively rather than selectively. The two probes answer different questions---whether
training a component lies on the interventional path to acquiring degree~4 versus where
the acquired computation resides---and only the freeze answer is clearly
optimizer-dependent here. That split does not contradict the depth threshold
(Proposition~D1): depth $\geq 2$ is required for degree-4 acquisition under both
optimizers, but which sub-network must be \emph{trained} to acquire it is
optimizer- and, under Muon, config-dependent. The freeze reversal likewise leaves the
acquisition-\emph{order} reading intact (primary-cell SI TOST; positive rank index in
24/24 scale-grid configurations). The optimizer-conditioned effect is confined to which
sub-network must be trained to complete the top degree and whether the unfrozen circuit
completes it at this difficulty pocket---a boundary condition on ownership, not on order
geometry. This is consistent with evidence that different optimizers can follow
qualitatively different training paths while reaching comparable task performance
\citep{nanda2023progress,xie2024adamw}; here those paths nonetheless deposit the
degree-4-selective computation at the same layer-1 MLP locus in point estimate (with the
$n{=}8$ interval scope above). Scope tags for both probes are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:evidence-ledger}.

\paragraph{Acquisition geometry is optimizer-equivalent (TOST); timing is not.}
The ownership dissociation is readable only because the acquisition geometry underneath
is optimizer-equivalent under the analysis-plan TOST. The staircase-index TOST (Section~\ref{sec:si}) is the cleanest
evidence: across AdamW, Muon, and SGDM, the per-degree order is equivalent within the
pre-specified $1.5$-SI SESOI for all three pairs at uncorrected $\alpha=0.05$
(Muon--AdamW $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}=0.044$, the marginal pair; Holm holds only for
AdamW--SGDM at this margin), and within a $\pm 2.0$ multiplicity-safe sensitivity margin
for all three pairs under Holm ($p\leq 0.006$). That ordering is what Proposition~G1
predicts: low-to-high acquisition reflects the Walsh-basis gradient-correlation structure
of the target, and no preconditioner can invent gradient signal along a direction where
the reachable gradient is zero. Separately, the induction timing grid
(Section~\ref{sec:timing}) shows Muon advancing emergence by $1.6$--$3.7\times$ over
AdamW and ${\sim}6.9$--$12\times$ over SGDM without reorganizing the staircase order
established above---orthogonalization as clock, not compass---consistent with
\citet{beneventano2026spectraldynamics} and with reports that optimizer or architecture
choice can accelerate grokking \citep{park2025kagrokking,lee2024grokfast}.

\paragraph{The subspace null and its limits.}
The induction-subspace result (Appendix~\ref{sec:subspace}) is the weakest leg. The
${\sim}3$-D effective update dimension is a robust positive under AdamW
(architecture-stable across context lengths; the PCA dimension itself is
optimizer-dependent, Appendix~\ref{sec:subspace}); it is consistent with the circuit-level
simplicity highlighted by \citet{olsson2022induction}. The ``directions not detected''
reading, however, requires the mapping from the disentangled ansatz of
\citet{musat2025inductionheads} into this architecture's parameter basis to be
correctly specified. The planted-direction self-test validates projector arithmetic but
not this mapping. We therefore report the result as ``we could not detect the ansatz
directions (mapping unvalidated)'' rather than ``the ansatz is wrong''; upgrading to a
strong claim would require planting the ansatz in a model that should exhibit it and
verifying recovery ($\text{capture} \approx 1$).

\paragraph{Relation to staircase / leap-complexity theory and prior work.}
The staircase property \citep{abbe2021staircase} and leap-complexity theory
\citep{abbe2023leap} predict that hierarchical target structure forces sequential
acquisition under gradient descent. Our results relate to this picture in four moves,
each marked as confirmation, extension, or only-consistent.

\emph{Confirmation.} The TOST equivalence (Section~\ref{sec:si}) confirms that
orthogonalizing updates (Muon) do not override the hierarchical gradient-seeding
mechanism described by Proposition~G1: the staircase is architecture-and-target--imposed,
not optimizer-imposed. The observed low-to-high degree order ($1\to4$) and cross-optimizer
SI equivalence (pre-specified $\pm1.5$ uncorrected for all pairs; $\pm2.0$ under
multiplicity correction) are a direct empirical confirmation that the acquisition order
is a property of the target's basis geometry, exactly the qualitative prediction of
\citet{abbe2021staircase,abbe2023leap}. We do not claim a formal theorem; the theory's
conditions are stated for gradient-descent-family optimizers, and we confirm empirically
that the prediction holds when those conditions are extended to Muon.

\emph{Extension.} The staircase / leap-complexity framework was not developed for
orthogonalizing optimizers. Our finding that Muon, an optimizer outside the gradient-descent
family assumed by the theory, preserves the sequential acquisition order is an empirical
extension: it suggests the hierarchical gradient-seeding mechanism is robust to
spectral preconditioners, not merely a gradient-descent artifact. This is consistent with
Proposition~G1's argument that the gradient signal along high-degree directions is
structurally zero until lower degrees are fitted, independent of the preconditioner.

\emph{Generalization of the Barak et al.\ picture.} \citet{barak2022hidden} study a
single-parity phase transition, one hidden-progress episode followed by a single
threshold crossing. Our degree-staircase generalizes this to a multi-degree sequential
staircase: $k=1\to4$ acquisition events whose ordering is fixed by target monomial
structure. The phase-transition intuition of \citet{barak2022hidden} is thus only-consistent
with our picture (both involve hidden progress until a threshold), while the sequential
multi-degree structure itself goes beyond their single-parity setting; we do not claim to
subsume their theory.

\emph{Depth threshold (only-consistent).} The depth-threshold (Proposition~D1) connects
to the interaction-order framing: the staircase's highest step (degree 4) requires the
composition of two mixing stages, which is an efficiency argument of the same family as
the results in \citet{sanford2023transformers}. This is an only-consistent positioning:
our empirical threshold is consistent with their depth-separation framing, but we observe
it as an efficiency gap rather than proving a new separation theorem.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}

The study is toy-scale (${\sim}400$k parameters, two layers; Boolean staircase + induction)
and is offered as a controlled, multi-seed dissociation with open logs---not as a
universal law of transformers or a new training algorithm. The lead freeze$\perp$ablation dissociation is scoped: freeze outcomes are
optimizer- and configuration-dependent (AdamW attention-necessity at $d=128$ tightens at
$L=24$, $d=256$; Muon two-path redundancy at $d=128$ reverses to an attention rescue at
that audit; SGDM freeze arms are fit-gated and uninformative), and ablation MLP L1
selectivity has wide CIs at $n=8$ (include zero in absolute terms; the Muon paired
contrast excludes zero). The geometry leg that makes the dissociation interpretable
(SI TOST and depth$\geq2$ on the staircase; induction timing ratios as a separate clock
probe) is established under three optimizers; the induction subspace is a bounded null,
not a positive dissociation pillar. The Section~\ref{sec:ownership} degree-4
``half-time $\to 0$'' under attention-freeze is a failed-to-learn artifact (degree-4
correlation $\approx 0$), so the result is a component-necessity (not ordering)
statement; the overall fit gap is not significant at $n = 15$. The
Appendix~\ref{sec:subspace} ansatz mapping construction could itself be the failure
point (the self-test rules out mechanical bugs, not a construction mismatch), so we say
``directions not detected'', not ``directions wrong''. Cell sizes are $n = 3$--$15$
depending on the arm. A 24-configuration grid ($L\in\{16,24,32\}\times
d\in\{256,512\}\times\text{depth}\in\{2,4\}$, both optimizers, 96 runs; 5 seeds per
$d=256$ cell, 3 seeds per $d=512$ cell) shows the staircase order surviving that scale
window: a positive mean rank index in 24/24 configurations ($+0.035$ to $+1.000$) and
AdamW/Muon rank-index sign agreement in 12/12 configurations (mean
$|\Delta\text{rank}|=0.265$, within seed noise)---a bounded robustness check, not a
scaling law. $L=24$ remains a genuine high-variance, bimodal-fit pocket rather than a
monotone scaling trend (mean of the per-cell median fit dips to $0.859$, with run-level
median $0.866$ and $2/32$ runs solved; mean rank dips to $0.263$; at $n=5$ per
optimizer the rank-index 95\% CIs still include 0 for both AdamW $[-0.08,+0.88]$ and
Muon $[-0.21,+0.58]$), so the bounded-robustness framing for that point stands
(Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}). Deeper or more complex tasks, where architecture depth,
width, and optimizer choice may interact non-trivially, remain future work. The work is
framed as emergence geometry rather than mechanistic interpretability: we attribute
where and when components are acquired, without reverse-engineering the learned
circuits themselves.

\subsection{Induction-subspace bounded null}\label{sec:subspace}

For completeness we report the program's weakest leg, a bounded null that does not bear on
the geometry--timing dissociation. Estimators are those of Section~\ref{sec:setup}:
captured fraction $\lVert P_{\mathcal{A}}\Delta\theta\rVert^{2}/\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert^{2}$
onto the three idealized induction directions
$\mathcal{A}=\{D_1,D_2,D_3\}$, and PCA effective dimension at $90\%$ variance. The
induction-emergence update is single-digit
dimensional at every context length (effective dimension ${\sim}3$, $L$-invariant and
architecture-stable), a robust positive. The variance captured along the idealized
3-parameter ansatz directions, however, is below the random-direction baseline ($0.0061$;
the captured fraction of a synthetic isotropic $\Delta$ under the same projector)
in 62 of 63 AdamW runs (45 main-grid $+$ 18 idealization-arm; median $0.00046$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:subspace}). The planted-direction self-test ($=1.0$) validates the projector
arithmetic but not the transport of the ansatz into this architecture's basis; if the
mapping is mis-specified, capture $\approx 0$ is the trivial expected outcome. We therefore
report this leg as ``we could not detect the ansatz directions (mapping unvalidated)'', not
a proven ``emergence lives elsewhere''. Across optimizers the result is an
optimizer-invariant null (AdamW, Muon, and SGDM alike, so it is not an AdamW artifact),
not a positive subspace dissociation: the repeat task saturates in-context learning at the
first evaluation (PCA dimension 1 at the point of solution for every optimizer), and the
Markov task, which shows genuine emergence, has no AdamW baseline and gives an
optimizer-dependent PCA dimension (Muon $\approx 5$ vs SGDM $\approx 2$ at matched
in-context learning). Upgrading this leg to a positive would require recovering the ansatz
from a planted-circuit model (capture $\approx 1$) first.
